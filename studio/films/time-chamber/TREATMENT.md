# LONGHOUR — treatment (style test: Exploded Teardown, film #7, Sun Nov 29)

One object the whole way: a sealed cabin with a ring around it, standing on the dark graphite seamless. Two clock faces are the visual anchor of every slot: one inside the cabin (02 CABIN CLOCK) and its twin on a post outside (01 REFERENCE CLOCK). Both are large analogue faces with a digital readout under them, and every trial number is shown on these faces, not only in the subtitles. Labels use the kit structure (accent two-digit index · name · one spec line, on a leader from a dot on the part). Accent: one cold cyan for the ring glow, dimension lines and the indices, so it reads differently from STILLWING's orange. 169 spoken words, 28 lines. Estimate: 73.5 s at 2.3 words/s, about 92-100 s with the visual holds (STILLWING ran 99.75 s with 186 words). The times below are estimates. Re-time them from the TTS.

**1. hook (0-7 s · a1-a3).** Frame 0 is the cover. The cabin is cut open on one side, both clocks are in frame, and the inside second hand visibly ticks slower than the outside one. Big text from frame 0: THIS CLOCK RUNS SLOW. / NOBODY TOUCHED IT. "Its twin, outside, keeps perfect time." The readouts show 12:00:07 outside and 12:00:05 inside, and the gap grows while you watch. No context before this.

**2. question (7-13 s · b1-b2).** "Broken? Or is time slower in there?" lands as a stamp at about 8 s: BROKEN? with SLOWER TIME? under it (learnings rule 1: the question comes inside 10 s). "Take it apart." The first separation: the cabin lifts into its five parts on one vertical axis, top first, 0.15 s apart. The parts are labelled only after the real beat, because the program slot names them.

**3. this_was_real (14-38 s · c1-c6). ONE dated beat, number first; the date appears only in the chip.** The camera drifts off the parts to two bare clock faces on a plain bench (drawn, generic, with no lab marks). One face rises on a dimension line `≈ 33 cm · about 1 foot`. "Lift a clock about a foot. It runs faster." "Measured, with two atomic clocks." Chip: `2010 · NIST · Science`. This is the only place a real name or date appears, and it stays well away from anything labelled LONGHOUR. **Visual payoff before any physics (the experiment):** the two faces drift apart on screen for about 2 s, the upper one ahead, with a counter `+0.000 000 000…`. Then the body-level lines: "Stand a couple of steps higher, and you age faster." (a drawn staircase with two figures) / "Over seventy-nine years, that adds up to about ninety billionths of a second." with the dimension plate `≈ 90 billionths of a second · 79 years`. Then a single plain physics pair: "Gravity slows time." / "Stronger gravity, slower clocks." The lower clock sinks into a shaded well. These are not two stacked technical lines but one idea in two short beats. The 15-25 s stretch holds only c1-c3: about a foot, atomic clocks, your own staircase. There is no jargon in it.

**4. program (39-54 s · d1-d3). Fiction.** Title card in screen space: LONGHOUR · FILE 07 (invented). "Longhour asks: what if you made the gravity?" The parts come back in and get their labels: `01 REFERENCE CLOCK · outside` · `02 CABIN CLOCK · inside` · `03 MASS RING · dense, spinning` · `04 PRESSURE SHELL` · `05 SEAT`. "A sealed cabin. A dense ring spins around it." The ring starts turning, with a cyan glow on its rim. Spec plate: "Spec: the cabin clock loses one second a day." `CABIN CLOCK · −1 s / day`. Everything here is invented. No agency, lab, university or person is named, and nothing real is placed next to the title.

**5. trials (55-71 s · e1-e4). Fiction, escalating; each trial opens on its number.** TRIAL 01: "Twenty-four hours. One second behind." The faces read 24:00:00 and 23:59:59, stamped CONFIRMED. TRIAL 02: "Ring at full speed. One hour behind." The ring blurs and the faces read 24:00 / 23:00, stamped CONFIRMED. TRIAL 03: "a volunteer." A plain silhouette takes 05 SEAT, with no face and no name. "One day passes inside. Outside, a week." The cabin face reads DAY 1 and the outside face flips DAY 1 → DAY 7 on a calendar strip, stamped CONFIRMED.

**6. verdict (71-95 s · f1-f8). Catch + stamp; fiction that agrees with the physics.** "One catch." Near-silence. "Inside, nothing feels slow." Cut inside the cabin: her watch, a pulse line and a thought bubble all tick at the same slow rate as the cabin clock. "Her watch, her pulse, her thoughts. All slow together." "She gets no extra time." "She just steps out into the future." The hatch opens and the outside calendar reads DAY 7. "There is no way back." An arrow on the outside clock is struck out where it would turn backwards. Near-silence, then the stamp thud: **CLEARED: ONE-WAY TRIP TO THE FUTURE.** (same twist shape as LODESTONE and STILLWING). "Longhour is cleared. As a one-way trip to the future." The narrator says this catch inside the fiction. It has no source chip (see FACTS.md "Dropped").

**7. outro (95-100 s · g1).** Fast pull-back: the parts close into the sealed cabin of frame 0 with its dimensions drawn on it, and the two clocks are still out of step. The right third stays unseen until this pull-back. End text: NEXT CASE SUNDAY / FOLLOW @compandgrow. "Next case Sunday. Follow comp and grow."
