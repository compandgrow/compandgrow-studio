# FACT #7 — the plane with no moving parts — facts

Fact short: 100% real, no fiction. Every card line must be a row below. (The STILLWING link line refers to our own fictional film, not to MIT.)

## Safe on screen (numbers)
- **5 m** wingspan
- **40,000 volts**
- **10** flights
- **2018**, **MIT**, **Nature** (published 21 Nov 2018)
- "no moving parts **in its engine/propulsion**", "nothing spins", "**nearly** silent"
- NOT on screen: any weight figure, any distance figure (60 m), "zero noise" — see Dropped.

## Dropped / changed
- **Weight ("about 2 kilos")** — sources disagree: MIT "about 5 pounds" (2.27 kg), AIP "2.45 kilograms". Neither rounds cleanly to "about 2 kg". Drop the weight from beat 1 (or say "lighter than a carry-on bag" — not sourced, so better just drop).
- **"It flew 60 metres"** — MIT says 60 m ("the maximum distance within the gym"), AIP says "roughly 55 meters per flight". Drop the number; say "across the gym".
- **"Zero noise"** in the hook — Nature says "nearly silently". Use "nearly silent". Suggested hook: "This plane flew. Its engine has zero moving parts." or "This plane flew. Nothing on it spins."
- **"Zero moving parts" for the whole plane** — sources say no moving parts *in the propulsion system*; keep the claim to the engine/propulsion.
- **"The idea came from watching Star Trek"** — source says "partly"; keep "partly".
- nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0707-9 was blocked (permission timeout); the Nature abstract was read via RePEc instead.
