# HULL 7 — Treatment

Style: Engineering Briefing Board. 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~90 s. English narration (Kokoro, bm_george), burned-in subtitles + .srt.
Played straight: a fictional program (HULL 7, an invented escort-class hull) briefed as a real engineering decision. No real ship names, hull numbers, agencies, document numbers or people. Real physics is used where it is real (degaussing, mirages, refraction); the leap to optical invisibility is the fiction.
No licensable photos are reachable from this workspace, so every object is a marker sketch on the board (STYLE.md §10: no photo → draw it in marker; never a generated image presented as real).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One measurement that keeps growing** — the distance at which an observer can no longer see the hull: 0 m → 500 m → 2 km → 8 km, each a dimension line ridden by the camera, until it passes the horizon. Strong rhythm, but the "how" gets squeezed.
2. **Before/after with a ghost** — the first plan (paint and smoke screens) is erased to a ghost; the coil plan is drawn over it. Good for "why the old way failed", weak payoff.
3. **Checklist down the wall** — a vertical briefing read top to bottom: the problem, the precedent, the principle, the build, the power, the tests, the side effect, the verdict; each item stamped as the camera moves down. The tall 9:16 frame suits a wall read downward, and the final pull-back reveals one tall poster.

**Chosen: 3, with 1 folded in as the test section.** A vertical wall read downward fits the phone; the tests are a growing dimension line (structure 1) inside the checklist. The opening question tag flips to its answer at the end.

## 2. Logline and arc

A ship is seen because light bounces off it; the navy already hides ships from magnets with coils of cable — Hull 7 pushes the same coils far enough to bend light around the hull, and the briefing walks it from sketch to sea trials.
- Setup: the problem (a hull is seen) and the precedent (degaussing hides it from magnetic mines).
- Turn: the principle (a mirage bends light through graded air) applied to the hull; the rays curve around it.
- Ending: tests pass at every range; one red side effect is noted; stamp CLEARED FOR SEA TRIALS; the opening tag "SEEN?" flips to "NOT SEEN"; pull back to the whole wall.

## 3. Benchmark

- A defence-procurement design review filmed on a wall of drawings (grammar: decision by evidence; plan above elevation).
- The ray-diagram pages of a school optics textbook (grammar: rays as straight blue lines that bend only at a boundary).
- Learn: one reading path down the wall; dimension lines as the units of proof; red kept for verdicts.
- Don't take: any real program, insignia, ship silhouette with a real hull number, layout or typography.
